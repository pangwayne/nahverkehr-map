2. Visiting this app

When you open the app, your browser sends technical data to my hosting provider Cloudflare: your IP address, date and time, the address requested (for example which stop's departures, or the start and destination stops and the time of a route search), the page you came from, and your browser and operating system. Cloudflare uses this data to deliver the app and to protect it from attacks. The photo in the header comes from my website www.pangwayne.com, which Cloudflare also hosts, so the same applies there.

Error logs: Cloudflare keeps logs of the requests to the app for me (among other things the address requested, the time and the answer), which help me find errors. They are deleted after 3 days.

Bot detection: Every time a page loads, Cloudflare runs a small check script in your browser that looks at technical features of the browser to detect automated access (bots). Cloudflare stores the result in the cookie cf_clearance on your device.

Protection against overload: So nobody can swamp the app with mass requests, my server counts per IP address how many departures and trips were newly fetched from Transitous (section 6) in the last minute, and refuses more once there are over 100; route searches have their own count, with a limit of 10. The counts only last that minute and are not stored.

Legal basis: my legitimate interest in a secure, working app (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). The bot detection and its cookie protect the app you are using and are strictly necessary for that (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG).

Recipient: Cloudflare, Inc., USA. Cloudflare is certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework.

Storage: The error logs are deleted after 3 days. Cloudflare deletes the other data once it is no longer needed for these purposes.

3. No tracking, no consent banner

The app itself sets no cookies and uses no analytics, advertising or tracking. That is why there is no cookie banner.

If you switch the language (DE / EN), your browser remembers the choice in its local storage (entry lang, value de or en). It stays on your device and is never sent to me or anyone else. You can delete it in your browser settings. It is strictly necessary for the setting you asked for (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG).

So the map does not jump back when you reload the page, your browser keeps the current map view in this tab's session storage (entry view: the centre point and zoom level of the map). If you used "My location", that view can be near where you were. It stays on your device, is never sent to me or anyone else, and is deleted as soon as you close the tab or browser. It is strictly necessary for the map you are using (Section 25(2) No. 2 TDDDG). Earlier versions of the app kept this entry in local storage for good; the app deletes that old copy on your next visit.

The app's own font is delivered from this site, not from an outside font service.

4. The map

The map images, map fonts and icons are loaded by your browser directly from OpenFreeMap. In doing so, your browser sends your IP address, the map area requested, the page you came from, and your browser and operating system to OpenFreeMap. According to OpenFreeMap, its normal access logs contain no IP addresses and it uses no cookies; error logs may contain your IP address and are deleted after 7 days. During a security incident (for example an attack), it may log IP addresses for up to 30 days. OpenFreeMap may deliver the map through Cloudflare's network; Cloudflare then receives the same technical data. Details: OpenFreeMap privacy policy.

Legal basis: my legitimate interest in showing a map without running my own map server (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Recipient: Hyperknot Software Kft., Hungary (OpenFreeMap), possibly through its service provider Cloudflare, Inc., USA (certified under the EU-US Data Privacy Framework).

5. "My location"

Using your location is optional. The app reads it only when you press My location (in German: Mein Standort), or the location button next to "From" in the route search (Nearest stop as start), once per press; it does not follow you. The first time, your browser asks whether the app may use your location; if you allowed it earlier, the browser may not ask again.

Your exact position is used only on your device to find the closest stops and to show it as a red dot on the map. The dot stays until you press the button again or reload the page; the position is kept only in the open page and not stored. It is not sent to me or to anyone else. However, two things can show roughly where you are:

  • The app loads the departures of the stops it found, so my server (Cloudflare, section 2) receives which stops you asked for, just as if you had tapped them on the map.
  • The map moves to your position, so your browser loads the map images for that area from OpenFreeMap (section 4).

In the route search, the button only fills in the stop closest to you as the start; the map does not move and nothing is loaded until you search. Only that stop is then sent with the search (section 6).

Legal basis: your consent, given by pressing one of these buttons and allowing location access (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR, Section 25(1) TDDDG). You can withdraw it at any time by removing the location permission for this site in your browser settings; this does not affect what happened before. Without it, you can still use the app by searching for stops or tapping them on the map.

6. Live departures and route search

Timetables, live departures and connections come from Transitous. My server fetches them on your behalf, so Transitous receives only what it needs to answer (which stop, which trip, or the stops and time of a route search), never your IP address or any other data about you.

The stop search runs in your browser on the stop list the app has already loaded. What you type is not sent to me or anyone else; only the stop you then pick is loaded, like a tap on the map (section 2). The same goes for the "From" and "To" fields of the route search.

Route search: when you press "Find connections" (in German: Verbindungen suchen), the app sends the start and destination stops you picked, the date and time, and whether you want to leave or arrive then, to my server, which asks Transitous for connections. "Earlier", "Later" and "Refresh" send the same search again. Nothing is sent while you fill in the form. I do not store your searches and the app keeps no search history; the answer is shown only in the open page. Like every request, the search is part of Cloudflare's technical data and error logs (section 2). So that several people asking the same thing at the same moment cause only one request to Transitous, my server keeps the answer for up to two minutes, without any information about who asked.

Legal basis: my legitimate interest in offering a working timetable and route search (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).
