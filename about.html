About the Public Transport Punctuality Check

Last updated: November 2026

The Public Transport Punctuality Check shows live how punctual buses, trams and S-Bahn trains in Bonn, Cologne and Düsseldorf are right now, worked out from public real-time data. It is an open-source personal project by Pang Wayne Wee, free to use with no sign-up.

Open the live map

What does the site show?

  • The buses, trams and S-Bahn trains in the public data on a map, colored by their current delay.
  • For each city, the share of vehicles on time and the median delay among late vehicles.
  • Bonn, Cologne and Düsseldorf side by side, also split by kind of vehicle.
  • Tram lines running late right now, and the vehicles of a line.
  • Live departures per stop and a route planner.

What does "on time" mean?

On time means here: 1 minute late at most. Early counts as on time. The colors stand for:

  • Green: 0-1 minute
  • Yellow: 2-5 minutes
  • Orange: 6-10 minutes
  • Red: 11+ minutes
  • Grey: no live data, timetable only

The color belongs to the vehicle, not the track: a red line is not a traffic jam but a late vehicle on its way.

How are the numbers worked out?

  • They describe the whole area inside each city's official limits, whatever the map shows, and are reloaded every minute.
  • The percentage counts only vehicles with live data. Under 10 of those, there is no percentage.
  • The median delay counts only the late vehicles (2+ minutes): half of them are less late, half more. It appears from 10 late vehicles on.
  • Reports of 60 minutes or more are left out as potentially unreliable; some may be genuine delays. These vehicles stay on the map but are not in any number, and a note says how many there are. In a big disruption this can make the on-time share look better than it is.

Which vehicles count?

Buses, trams (tram, Stadtbahn, U-Bahn) and S-Bahn trains of the operators in the three cities, such as KVB in Cologne, Stadtwerke Bonn (SWB) and Rheinbahn in Düsseldorf. Regional trains (RB, RE) show as pale circles on the map but are not counted: a late train passing through would otherwise move a whole city's numbers. The same goes for rail replacement buses named after a train line.

Are the vehicle positions real?

No, they are estimates, worked out from the timetable and the live times, not GPS. If 40% of the time between two stops has passed, the dot sits 40% along that stretch. The dots move every second; new data comes every 1-2 minutes.

What do the numbers not tell you?

They are a snapshot, not a punctuality statistic. They cannot tell you whether one city is generally more punctual than another. No historical transit data is collected or stored. Cancellations are not counted: a missing dot does not mean a trip is cancelled. All information without guarantee.

Where does the data come from?

Live data and timetables come from Transitous, a free, open-source service, and its data sources. Map data © OpenStreetMap contributors, map: OpenFreeMap. The source code is on GitHub (MIT license).
